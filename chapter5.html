Question 1

In a circle, a chord is 5 cm away from the centre. If the radius of the circle is 13 cm, what is the length of the chord?

Diagram

Solution

Let AB be the chord and OM be the perpendicular from the centre.

Since the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord,

AM = MB

In right △OMA,

OA² = OM² + AM²

13² = 5² + AM²

169 = 25 + AM²

AM² = 144

AM = 12 cm

Therefore,

AB = 2 × 12 = 24 cm

Answer: 24 cm

Question 2

An arc of a circle subtends an angle of 70° at the centre. What is the measure of the angle subtended by the same arc at a point on the circle?

Diagram

Solution

The angle subtended by an arc at the centre is twice the angle subtended at any point on the remaining part of the circle.

∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB

Given,

∠AOB = 70°

∠ACB = 70° ÷ 2

∠ACB = 35°

Answer: 35°

Question 3

The diameter of a circle is 26 cm. A chord of length 24 cm is drawn in the circle. Find the distance from the centre of the circle to the chord.

Diagram

Solution

Radius of the circle,

r = 26 ÷ 2 = 13 cm

Let OM be the perpendicular from the centre to the chord AB. A perpendicular from the centre of a circle bisects the chord.

AM = MB = 24 ÷ 2 = 12 cm

In right triangle OMA,

OA² = OM² + AM²

13² = OM² + 12²

Question 4

A circle has a radius of 15 cm. A chord is drawn. The distance from the centre of the circle to the chord is 9 cm. What is the length of the chord?

Diagram

OA² = OM² + AM²

AM² = 144

AM = 12 cm

Question 5

Prove that the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre of the circle.

Diagram

Solution

Let AB be a chord of the circle and let M be the midpoint of the chord. Draw the line through M perpendicular to AB. We have to prove that this line passes through the centre O.

Join OA and OB.

Since OA = OB (radii of the same circle), triangle OAB is an isosceles triangle.

In an isosceles triangle, the line drawn from the vertex to the midpoint of the base is perpendicular to the base.

OA = OB

AM = MB

∴ OM ⟂ AB

Therefore, the perpendicular bisector of the chord AB passes through the centre O of the circle.

Hence Proved. ∎

Question 6

The diameter of a circle is AB. Point C is on the circumference. What is the measure of ∠ACB? Explain your reasoning.

Diagram

Solution

Since AB is the diameter of the circle, the angle subtended by the diameter at any point on the circumference is always a right angle. This is known as the Angle in a Semicircle Theorem.

∠AOB = 180°

The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference standing on the same arc.

∠AOB = 2 × ∠ACB

180° = 2 × ∠ACB

∠ACB = 180° ÷ 2

∠ACB = 90°

Answer: ∠ACB = 90°.

Question 7

ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral inscribed in a circle. If ∠A = 75°, what is the measure of ∠C? If ∠B = 110°, what is the measure of ∠D?

Diagram

Solution

In a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of each pair of opposite angles is 180°.

∠A + ∠C = 180°

∠B + ∠D = 180°

Finding ∠C

∠A = 75°

∠C = 180° − 75°

∠C = 105°

Finding ∠D

∠B = 110°

∠D = 180° − 110°

∠D = 70°

Final Answers:

∠C = 105°
∠D = 70°

Question 8

Quadrilateral PQRS is inscribed in a circle. If ∠P = (2x + 10)° and ∠R = (3x − 20)°, find the value of x and the measures of ∠P and ∠R.

Diagram

Solution

In a cyclic quadrilateral, opposite angles are supplementary.

∠P + ∠R = 180°

(2x + 10) + (3x − 20) = 180

5x − 10 = 180

5x = 190

x = 38

Finding ∠P

∠P = 2 × 38 + 10

∠P = 76 + 10 = 86°

Finding ∠R

∠R = 3 × 38 − 20

∠R = 114 − 20 = 94°

Final Answer:

x = 38
∠P = 86°
∠R = 94°

Question 9

The distance of a chord of length 16 cm from the centre of a circle is 6 cm. Find the radius of the circle.

Diagram

Solution

Let AB be the chord and OM be the perpendicular from the centre to the chord.

The perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord.

AM = MB = 16 ÷ 2 = 8 cm

In right triangle OMA,

OA² = OM² + AM²

OA² = 6² + 8²

OA² = 36 + 64

OA² = 100

OA = √100 = 10 cm

Since OA is the radius of the circle,

Answer: Radius of the circle = 10 cm.

Question 10

A cyclic quadrilateral has sides 5, 5, 12, 12 units. Find its area.

Diagram

Solution

Since the quadrilateral is cyclic, we use Brahmagupta's Formula.

Area = √[(s − a)(s − b)(s − c)(s − d)]

where

s = (a + b + c + d) / 2

Here,

a = 5,  b = 5,  c = 12,  d = 12

s = (5 + 5 + 12 + 12) / 2

s = 34 / 2 = 17

Therefore,

Area = √[(17−5)(17−5)(17−12)(17−12)]

= √(12 × 12 × 5 × 5)

= √3600

= 60 square units

Answer: Area = 60 square units.

Question 20

A quadrilateral MNOP is inscribed in a circle. If MN is a diameter, what can you say about ∠MOP and ∠MNP? Explain your reasoning.

Diagram

Solution

Since MN is the diameter of the circle, any angle subtended by the diameter at a point on the circumference is a right angle.

The angle ∠MOP is subtended by the diameter MN. Therefore,

∠MOP = 90°

Similarly, the angle ∠MNP is also an angle standing on the diameter MP in the given figure, and hence it is also a right angle.

∠MNP = 90°

Thus, both angles are right angles because an angle subtended by a diameter at the circumference is always 90°.

Final Answer:

∠MOP = 90°
∠MNP = 90°

Question 21

Let ABCD be a cyclic quadrilateral. Explain why the exterior angle at any vertex is equal to the interior opposite angle (e.g., ∠CDE = ∠ABC, where E is a point on the extension of side CD).

Diagram

Solution

In a cyclic quadrilateral, the sum of a pair of opposite interior angles is 180°.

∠ABC + ∠BCD = 180°

Since CE is produced to point E, the interior angle ∠BCD and the exterior angle ∠CDE form a linear pair.

∠BCD + ∠CDE = 180°

From the above two equations,

∠ABC + ∠BCD = ∠BCD + ∠CDE

∴ ∠ABC = ∠CDE

Hence, the exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral is equal to its interior opposite angle.

Hence Proved. ∎

Question 22

“There is no chord of a circle that is longer than its diameter.” How do you justify this statement?

Diagram

Solution

Let AB be the diameter of a circle and CD be any other chord.

The perpendicular drawn from the centre O to the chord CD bisects the chord.

The length of a chord depends on its distance from the centre. As the distance of a chord from the centre increases, its length decreases.

The diameter is the only chord that passes through the centre of the circle. Hence, its distance from the centre is 0, which is the minimum possible distance.

Therefore, the diameter has the greatest possible length among all the chords of a circle.

Length of any chord ≤ Length of the diameter

Hence, there is no chord of a circle that is longer than its diameter.

Hence Proved. ∎
